# Visual Style & Page Design Guide — Elseframe / Beyonders

---

## 1. Dominant Panel with Inserts — Visual Rule

When a page uses a **dominant panel with inserts**, the page must be built around **one large primary image** that occupies most of the composition and establishes the main space, action, or emotional state.

### Role of Insert Panels
Insert panels are smaller, secondary windows placed over, inside, or along the dominant composition.

> **Key Rule:** Their function is **NOT** to replace the main image, but to complement it by showing:
> - Reaction
> - Close detail
> - Point of view
> - Gesture
> - Object
> - Micro-action
> - Zoom in or zoom out
> - Emotional clarification

### Composition Guidelines
- **Hierarchy:** The dominant image must remain visually readable as the page’s main event.
- **Integration:** Inserts must feel integrated into the same page logic, not like an unrelated separate grid.
- **When to Use:**
  - One image emotionally or spatially dominates the page.
  - The scene also needs one or more closer beats for clarity or emphasis.
- **Restraint:** Do not overcrowd the page with too many inserts. Use only the number needed to sharpen the dominant image.

---

## 2. Page Design Fundamentals

### Purpose
Design the comic page **before** writing the final generation prompt. Do not begin by choosing a panel count.

**First determine:**
1. What moment deserves to be shown.
2. Who emotionally owns the page.
3. What changes during the page.
4. What visual device best communicates that change.

### Core Rules
- **Emotional Ownership:** Every page must have an emotional owner.
- **Progression:** Every page must show progression.
- **Information Value:** Every panel must add change, consequence, reaction, or new information.
- **No Redundancy:** Do not repeat the same instant from several angles without narrative purpose.
- **No Default Grids:** Avoid automatic repetitive grids. **Do NOT default to five equal horizontal panels.**

---

## 3. Panel Dynamics

### Panel Orientation

| Orientation | Status | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vertical** | **Default** | Character acting, tension, falling, pursuit, body language, progression, travel through depth, natural vertical reading flow. |
| **Horizontal** | **Exception** | Establishing geography, monumental scale, long distance, pause / breathing room, face-to-face confrontation, lateral movement. |

### Panel Count & Narrative Rhythm
Panel count follows rhythm, not a fixed template:

- **1 Panel:** Splash, monumental reveal, major arrival, climax, transformation, iconic image.
- **2 Panels:** Strong contrast, before / after, question / consequence.
- **3 Panels:** Setup $\rightarrow$ Change $\rightarrow$ Consequence.
- **4 Panels:** Clear, versatile narrative structure.
- **5–6 Panels:** Complex interaction, chase, escalating action, travel sequence, conversation with several meaningful beats.
- **7+ Panels:** Only for deliberate compression, simultaneity, or controlled chaos.

---

## 4. Visual Devices

Every page must choose **at least one deliberate dominant visual device**. Choose only what supports the main beat—do not use all of them at once.

### Device Guide & Usage

* **Dominant Panel + Inserts**  
  *Use when:* One image defines the page and smaller details clarify emotion, reaction, or mechanism.
* **Bleeding / Border Break**  
  *Use for:* Physical intrusion, impact, sudden motion, or a moment that should feel larger than its panel.
* **Open Panel / White Background**  
  *Use for:* Shock, impact, suspension, or emotional rupture.
* **Irregular Panels**  
  *Use for:* Instability, violence, panic, or fragmented experience.
* **Spotting Blacks**  
  *Use for:* Threat, mystery, focus, visual weight, or information withholding.
* **Selective Cross-Hatching**  
  *Use for:* Material shadow, tension, or atmosphere. *(Never as uniform texture).*
* **Extreme Close-Up**  
  *Use when:* One small detail carries narrative meaning (e.g., a hand, mask mechanism, eye, object, restraint, control).
* **Negative Space**  
  *Use for:* Isolation, scale, exhaustion, anticipation, or awe.
* **Silhouette**  
  *Use when:* Shape and presence matter more than detail.
* **Progressive Reduction**  
  *Use when:* A page moves from broad context into increasingly personal or claustrophobic tension.
* **Extreme Scale Contrast**  
  *Use when:* Characters or ships confront the massive size of the Aureon system *(frequent in Beyonders)*.
* **Foreground / Background Counterpoint**  
  *Use for:* Layering narrative actions and simultaneous character responses.
* **Debris as Directional Vector**  
  *Use for:* Guiding the reader's eye and conveying explosive kinetic movement.

## 5. Beyonders Aesthetic & Tone

### General Aesthetic
- Adventurous cosmic scale.
- Strong, readable silhouettes.
- Environmental storytelling.
- Expressive character acting & moments of physical comedy.
- Clear spatial geography.
- Contrast between tiny characters and enormous worlds, ships, or structures.
- Large environmental reveals.

### Lighting & Atmosphere Policy

> [!NOTE]
> **Beyonders is not permanently noir.** Do NOT make every page dark. Shadow must narrate, not simply darken.

- **Darkness & Spotting Blacks:** Reserve for pirate interiors, Imperial threat, Astronauta mystery, dangerous wilderness, and emotional tension.
- **Open Space & Large Horizons:** Use atmospheric color, open lighting, and broad expanses for planetary exploration, travel, discovery, awe, relief, and comedy.

---

## 6. Pacing & Variety Rules

- **No Repetition:** Do not repeat the same page architecture on consecutive pages unless repetition itself has narrative meaning.
- **Earned Splashes:** Splash and semi-splash images must be earned.
- **Deliberate Alternation:** Regularly alternate between:
  - Dominant panel + inserts
  - Vertical traversal
  - 3–4 panel cinematic pages
  - Irregular action layouts
  - Extreme close-up pages
  - Open landscape pages
  - Reveal pages
  - Foreground / background acting pages

---

## 7. Step-by-Step Design Order

Before writing each final prompt, determine in order:

1. **Narrative Function**
2. **Emotional Owner**
3. **Emotional Progression**
4. **Dominant Visual Device**
5. **Number of Meaningful Beats**
6. **Panel Count**
7. **Panel Hierarchy**
8. **Orientation of Each Panel**
9. **Beat-by-Beat Progression**
10. **Continuity**
11. **Information Retained**
12. **Restrictions**

---

## 8. Text & Dialogue Restrictions

> [!CAUTION]
> ### Strict Text Rules
> - **NO character dialogue.**
> - **NO speech balloons or thought balloons.**
> - **Narration Captions:** Allowed **only** when exact text is explicitly provided in the prompt.
> - **Sound Effects:** Allowed **only** when explicitly provided.
> - **Language:** Any written narration or linguistic sound effect must be in **neutral Latin American Spanish**.
> - **No Extra Text:** Do not invent additional text. No readable interface text, alien writing, signage, or labels unless explicitly requested.

---

## 9. Quality Verification: Minimum Page Test

Before finalizing, verify that the page design answers these 5 questions:

- [ ] **What changes?**
- [ ] **Who experiences that change?**
- [ ] **Which image dominates?**
- [ ] **What visual device carries the page?**
- [ ] **Why is this structure better than a standard grid?**

---

## 10. Summary Cheat Sheet

* **Avoid Rigid Grids:** Never default to 5 equal horizontal strips. Design from narrative function and emotional progression.
* **Orientation:** Vertical is default; horizontal is for scale, pause, distance, or lateral sweeps.
* **Visual Devices:** Always select at least one intentional visual device per page.
* **Panel Progression:** Every panel must add new information, reaction, or consequence.
* **Hierarchy:** Scale and shape panel layouts according to dramatic weight.
* **Visual Identity:** Balance cosmic scale and graphic shadow masses with readable acting and clear environmental depth.
